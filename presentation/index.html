Flow cytometry and computational modelling

Asymmetric Partitioning and Cell Cycle Analysis in Jurkat Cells

How much of the variability between tumour cells comes from stochastic division and size control rather than from genetic mutations? Quantitative flow cytometry and mathematical models applied to a human T-cell lymphoma line.

Elisabetta Agnello
1 · Experimental protocols

Thawing protocol

Cryopreserved Jurkat cells were thawed, washed by centrifugation (7 min at 1400 rpm) to remove the cryoprotectant (DMSO), and resuspended in RPMI medium enriched with fetal bovine serum, which provides growth factors.

2 · Flow cytometry

Fluorescence

A fluorophore absorbs a photon and is promoted to an excited electronic state. After non-radiative vibrational relaxation it returns to the ground state by emitting a photon of lower energy, and therefore longer wavelength. This difference, the Stokes shift, lets the emitted light be separated spectrally from the excitation light.

  • Born–Oppenheimer approximation
  • Franck–Condon principle
  • Stokes shift
2 · Flow cytometry

The flow cytometer

Cells in suspension are focused by a sheath fluid and cross a laser beam one at a time. Forward scatter (FSC), collected along the beam, comes from diffraction around the cell and is used as a proxy of its size, keeping in mind that the relation is not linear. Side scatter (SSC), collected at 90°, comes from internal structures and measures granularity.

Fluorescence is split by wavelength onto photomultipliers (PMTs). The FACSAria sorter can also charge and deflect cells to isolate subpopulations.

2 · Flow cytometry

Staining protocol

To follow divisions, cells were incubated for 20 min at 37 °C with two dyes, then washed in PBS with 2% serum:

  • CellTrace Yellow labels the cytoplasm and is diluted at every division.
  • MitoFarRed labels the mitochondria.
2 · Flow cytometry

Sorting generation 0

The stained population was heterogeneous, so FACS was used to isolate only the cells with the highest and most uniform fluorescence: generation 0. A narrow starting distribution keeps the peaks of later generations separated and gives a population more uniform in morphology and cell-cycle stage.

Samples were then measured three times a day (morning, midday, afternoon) for three days.

2 · Flow cytometry

DAPI staining

At every time point an aliquot was fixed by adding it dropwise to ethanol at 4 °C while vortexing, which stops biological processes and permeabilises the membrane. On the last day the samples were stained with DAPI, which binds DNA, together with the detergent Triton X-100, to quantify the DNA content of each cell.

3 · Computational analysis

Gating strategy

Before any analysis the data were cleaned to keep only single, living cells. In the forward scatter (FSC-A) vs side scatter (SSC-A) plane, cells were separated from debris and electronic noise, which sit at low scatter values, and from aggregates at high values.

3 · Computational analysis

Gaussian deconvolution of generations

CellTrace Yellow halves at every division, so on a log2 scale the generations appear as equally spaced peaks moving to the left over time. A Gaussian mixture model splits each histogram into one Gaussian per generation, giving the fraction, mean and variance of every generation.

3 · Computational analysis

Asymmetric binomial model

Division gives the mother's content to one daughter with probability p and to the other with q = 1 − p: p = 0.5 is a perfectly symmetric division. The mean always halves; the variance depends on p.

3 · Computational analysis

The cell cycle

DNA labelled with DAPI separates three phases:

  • G1: diploid DNA content (2n), the first peak at low fluorescence.
  • S: DNA synthesis, with a content between 2n and 4n, spread between the two peaks.
  • G2: duplicated genome (4n), the second peak at about twice the G1 intensity.
3 · Computational analysis

Gating of DNA content

Aggregates are removed first, in the DAPI signal area vs FSC plane. Doublets, two cells crossing the laser together, are then removed by comparing the area and the width of the DAPI pulse, together with electronic noise.

3 · Computational analysis

Evolution of the generation fractions

Before studying size control we checked that the culture was in balanced growth. The fractions of cells in each generation evolve regularly and predictably, confirming a homeostatic state.

3 · Computational analysis

Size control mechanisms

Size control is studied through the added size: the size a cell adds during its cycle, compared with its size at birth. The slope m of their linear relation distinguishes the three classic models.

  • Sizer (m = −1): cells grow to a fixed critical size, so cells born smaller must add much more mass than cells born larger.
  • Timer (m > 0): cells grow for a fixed time, so the final size is proportional to the initial one.
  • Adder (m = 0): cells add a roughly constant amount of mass, uncorrelated with the initial mass.

3 · Computational analysis

Logarithmic growth factor

Growth is quantified from the ratio of the mean FSC in G2 and G1. Assuming the volume doubles between G1 and G2, α relates FSC to volume and defines a linearised size D, proportional to the cube root of the volume.

Conclusions

Conclusions

  • Partitioning of the total cytoplasm is a stochastically symmetric process: p ≈ 0.5.
  • This indirectly supports the hypothesis that the asymmetry of other organelles, such as mitochondria, is driven by active, regulated mechanisms.
  • Size control follows an adder-like mechanism: cells add a relatively constant mass increment at every cycle, a compensatory strategy that maintains size homeostasis.
  • Growth does not happen at a constant rate but accelerates through the phases of the cycle: βG1 < βS < βG2.

Both main results agree with the literature.

Conclusions

Limitations

  • Methodological: forward scatter is used as a proxy of cell size. It is correlated with size but is not a direct measure of volume, and other parameters can affect its intensity.
  • Experimental: the number of time points is necessarily limited, because each measurement removes a significant fraction of the culture.
